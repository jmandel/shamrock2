Play like a clover champ ☘

Writing your clover

  • Hook both words, evenly. Every clue links both words on its leaf; the trick is a link that’s strong for each. For climbing and clothing, “harness” beats “rope”: you climb in one and wear it. Traditionally a clue is a single word, in lowercase unless it’s a name or title (a capital is a hint!).
  • Think sideways. Links come in lots of flavours: phrases (fire + fly → “firefly”), categories (both are fish), examples (Apollo is a god and a moon mission), shared traits (both red), parts of a whole, relations (tool and user, cause and effect), places you’d find both, names and titles. The best clue often hooks each word a different way.
  • Scan all sixteen. Every card has four words, and any of them could swing round to face your leaf. Before you commit, check your clue against every word on your board. If it fits another pair just as well, find a sharper one.
  • Sharp beats clever. Go for the link your friends will actually make. Deep cuts and inside jokes are booby traps.
  • Think in corners. Each card sits between two leaves. If one clue has to be a little loose, make its neighbour crisp, so the card is pinned from both sides.
  • Pick decoys by nerve. Shaky clues? One decoy. Feeling bold? Throw in more.

Guessing a clover

  • Grab the giveaway. Find the clue with an obvious pair and lock it in first.
  • Brainstorm the links. For a pair you’re eyeing, run through the flavours: a phrase? a category? a famous name? Whatever you find, ask if it’s the clue on the leaf.
  • Be the writer. For any pair you’re eyeing, ask: “Would I have written this clue for these two words?”
  • Ask “why not the obvious one?” If a pair has a far more natural clue, wonder why the writer skipped it. Spot another word on the board that would have made the obvious clue risky? That’s your confirmation. Can’t find a reason? Rethink that pair.
  • Check both sides. A card in a corner faces two leaves. Turn it in your head until both outward words fit their clues.
  • Sniff out the decoy. A card that fits nothing comfortably is probably a stranger. Leave it in the tray.
  • Bank your wins. Cards that stay after a Guess are locked in. Use their words to crack the neighbours.
  • Don’t overthink. Most clues mean just what they say. Hunt for twists only when the simple reading breaks.
